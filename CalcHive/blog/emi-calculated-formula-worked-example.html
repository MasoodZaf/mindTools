How is EMI Calculated? Formula with a Worked Example

By Masood Zafar · 2026-11-05

Understanding how EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment) is calculated can help you make better decisions when taking out loans. This post explains the formula and provides a step-by-step worked example.

What is EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It's the fixed payment you make every month towards repaying a loan, which covers both principal and interest.

The EMI Calculation Formula

The standard formula to calculate EMI is:

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n – 1]

Where:

  • P = Principal loan amount
  • r = Monthly interest rate (annual interest rate divided by 12 and by 100)
  • n = Number of monthly payments (loan tenure in months)

Worked Example: Calculating EMI

Suppose you borrow a principal amount (P) of 100,000 units at an annual interest rate of 12% for 2 years (24 months).

Let's calculate the EMI step by step:

  1. Calculate the monthly interest rate (r):
    • Annual rate = 12% per year
    • Monthly rate (r) = 12 / 12 / 100 = 0.01
  2. Total number of payments (n):
    • 2 years = 24 monthly instalments
  3. Plug values into the EMI formula:
    • P = 100,000
    • r = 0.01
    • n = 24
EMI = [100,000 × 0.01 × (1 + 0.01)^24] / [(1 + 0.01)^24 – 1]

Calculate (1 + 0.01)^24:

  • (1 + 0.01)^24 ≈ 1.2697346

Calculate numerator:

  • 100,000 × 0.01 × 1.2697346 = 1,269.7346

Calculate denominator:

  • 1.2697346 – 1 = 0.2697346

So,

EMI = 1,269.7346 / 0.2697346 ≈ 4,707.35

Your monthly EMI payment would be approximately 4,707.35 units every month for 24 months. Over the whole loan you repay about 112,976.40 units, of which about 12,976.40 is interest.
